    Spanish-language documents that accompany many of them in the archives. Each document is presented
    with its page images, what the project knows about it, and — where that work has been done — a
    transcription you can read alongside the original.

  <h2 class="md:text-2xl text-xl font-medium title-font text-text-dark mt-10 mb-4">FAQs</h2>

  <h3 class="text-lg font-bold title-font text-text-dark mt-8 mb-2">What is available in the Ticha Digital Text Explorer?</h3>
  <p>
    Handwritten documents from Oaxacan archives — testaments, bills of sale, land deeds and descriptions,
    petitions, receipts, and complaints — alongside a small number of printed colonial books, including
    grammars and doctrinal texts. Every document has page images you can zoom and page through, and
    metadata recording the archive, call number, town, year, and the people named in it. Transcriptions
    are available for a growing portion of the collection.
  </p>

  <h3 class="text-lg font-bold title-font text-text-dark mt-8 mb-2">What is a Ticha Digital Edition?</h3>
  <p>
    A digital edition is a document the project has selected for more focused editorial work. It opens on
    the original page images and may also have a transcription or a link to a Spanish translation. Digital
    editions are marked with a flower, both in search results and in the
    <a href="/explore/editions">list of editions</a>. Because this work is done document by document, the
    material available varies from one edition to the next.
  </p>

  <h3 class="text-lg font-bold title-font text-text-dark mt-8 mb-2">Why do some documents appear twice, in Zapotec and in Spanish?</h3>
  <p>
    Many Zapotec documents are accompanied in the archive by a Spanish translation. The two are kept here
    as separate records, so that each can be read against its own images, and each links to the other from
    the top of its page. A record whose title begins with <em>Translation of</em> is the Spanish document.
  </p>

  <h3 class="text-lg font-bold title-font text-text-dark mt-8 mb-2">Why do some documents have no transcription?</h3>
  <p>
    Transcribing colonial handwriting is slow, careful work, and it is still under way. A document without a
    transcription is still fully available as images. If you would like to help with transcription, or you
    have worked on one of these documents already,
    <a href="https://ticha.haverford.edu/en/form/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">get in touch ↗</a>.
  </p>

  <h3 class="text-lg font-bold title-font text-text-dark mt-8 mb-2">May I reuse the images and transcriptions?</h3>
  <p>
    The images are published here by permission of the archives that hold the documents. Where a permission
    letter exists, it is linked from the document page under <em>Archive permissions</em>. Please start
    there, and see <a href="/connect/credits">Credits and Acknowledgements</a> for how to cite Ticha and the
    individual documents.
  </p>
